Blackstone Turns Computing Into an Asset Class

Blackstone has built a decade of data center bets into a $200B empire, and President Jon Gray now calls AI the firm's most important undertaking.

By the numbers: Blackstone's data center investments, counting debt and projects under construction, reached $200B in July, up from $55B in June 2024. For scale, its entire real estate book was last valued at $608B, making compute a fast-rising slice of the portfolio.

An accelerating bet: The firm leaned in with a roughly $10B purchase of QTS in 2021. The land QTS held was expected to last a decade, yet it was used up about 18 months after closing once ChatGPT set off a demand surge.

Beyond the buildings: The bet no longer stops at buildings. It now runs from server halls into chip financing, power generation and stakes in AI labs, and a 2023 loan to CoreWeave, backed by a Microsoft contract, helped create the entire market for chip-backed lending.

Powering the load: The electricity math is daunting. To feed the demand, Blackstone is buying into wind, solar, batteries and gas plants, infrastructure chief Sean Klimczak said, as BloombergNEF estimates these facilities will draw about 20% of US electricity by 2035, up from 5.9% today.

Spreading the chips: The firm has taken positions in Anthropic, SpaceX and OpenAI through its BXPE vehicle, partnered with Google on a cloud venture, and joined five other financial giants pledging $500B to fund Nvidia customers.

The bear case: Not everyone is convinced. Jeffrey Hooke of Johns Hopkins told Bloomberg the reliance on hyperscaler contracts is unproven, warning that tenants could decline to renew if demand softens and that packing these assets into retail funds could backfire.

➥ THE TAKEAWAY

What's at stake: Blackstone is wagering that computing capacity becomes an asset class as durable as warehouses or apartments. If the buildout holds, the payoff reshapes where real estate capital flows; if it stalls, that same concentration cuts the other way.

✍️ Editor’s Picks

  • Still building when others aren't: While higher rates and tighter financing have sidelined developments, Highgates continues moving projects forward. See what's behind their approach. (sponsored)

  • CLO debut: Castlelake is entering the CRE CLO market with a $1.1B deal backed by bridge loans on 26 properties, as private credit crowds into floating-rate lending.

  • Claude now sources CRE deals: Terrakotta's Claude Agent is disrupting the CRE industry as we speak. Brokers can now automate LLC skip-tracing, find motivated sellers, and source off-market deals. (sponsored)

  • To the rescue: Rescue capital is easier to land than sponsors expect but quietly costs them control, a King & Spalding attorney told GlobeSt.

  • Wealth split: Median net worth for households under 35 fell 23% from 2022 to 2025 while those 75 and older gained 37%, a Fed study found, splitting renter demand into separate segments.

  • Storm exposure: Hurricane Isaias put $3.27B of securitized Florida CRE debt in its path, Trepp estimates, with apartments making up nearly 69% of balances across 15 counties.

🏘️ Multifamily

  • Apartment bonds: Rithm's Genesis Capital closed a $375M apartment-loan securitization, its first since 2021, as quarterly originations jumped to $637M from $160M in early 2024.

  • Debt reset: Dallas-based S2 Capital restructured $1.2B of debt across 26 apartment communities, a dozen in foreclosure, as Sun Belt owners wrestle with heavy supply and higher rates.

  • Volume spike: Rolling apartment sales hit $208.1B through Q3, the most since early 2023, though the AvalonBay–Equity Residential merger into Vivmark drove most of the 39.8% annual jump.

  • Rich renters: Millionaire renter households jumped 229% from 2020 to 2024, as affluent millennials and Gen Z increasingly choose leasing over owning.

  • Thin margins: Apartment owners kept just 11 cents of every rent dollar in 2025 as operating expenses rose 4.6%, outpacing revenue growth of 2.7% and squeezing margins across market-rate and affordable housing.

🏭 Industrial

  • Platform deal: EQT agreed to buy Stockbridge, a San Francisco manager overseeing about $16.5B of fee-earning assets and $27B of gross value, pushing EQT Real Estate deeper into industrial outdoor storage and manufactured housing.

  • Tenant buys: Michelin bought its 2M SF Wilmington, Illinois distribution hub for $151M from Silver Creek and GFH Partners, taking ownership of a super-regional facility that handles 35% of its US imported inventory.

  • Storage refi: Sitex Group secured a $39.4M loan from Lake Forest Bank to refinance its 147K SF Queens industrial outdoor storage property, anchored by a 20-year FDNY lease.

🏬 Retail

  • Store playbook: Walmart is turning its stores into fulfillment hubs and courting younger shoppers, with nearly 70% of 18-to-24-year-olds already buying from the chain.

  • Lunch gains: Convenience chains with the biggest lunch share are gaining traffic this year, with Buc-ee's and Casey's leading as high gas prices steer diners toward cheaper meals.

  • Younger diners: Chipotle visits rose 6% in Q3 2026, outpacing fast-casual peers as loyalty programs and promotions attracted younger diners while maintaining an affluent customer base.

  • Dining sale: Gen Korean BBQ's parent is selling restaurants to the Chubby Group, handing the multi-brand operator a bigger national footprint.

🏢 Office

  • Trophy trade: Zara founder Amancio Ortega's Pontegadea paid about $550M for Chicago's One North Wacker, one of the year's largest US office trades and a fresh benchmark for trophy towers.

  • Focus design: A new office-design framework argues firms can rebuild focus by giving each space one clear purpose, as workers grow three times more distracted than in pre-iPhone days.

  • Wellness loan: Terra secured a $183.8M refinancing from Monroe Capital for its 96K SF Bay Harbor Islands office complex, which is 90% leased and includes Tom Brady’s family office.

  • Lab default: A new lender is moving to foreclose on a stalled 156K SF Manhattan lab-and-office site near Moynihan Train Hall, where the owner defaulted on a $54M mortgage now totaling $65.9M.

🏨 Hospitality

  • Sideline play: Chicago’s decision to skip hosting the 2026 World Cup paid off as displaced conferences and business travelers fueled a hotel boom.

  • Hotel swap: Archer Capital Group acquired the 111-room Holiday Inn Express & Suites Fort Myers East for $11.1M from American Hotel Income Properties REIT in a 1031 exchange.

  • Convention build: Roanoke, TX is breaking ground on a city-owned convention center paired with a 205-key hotel, a roughly $97.5M project due to open in 2028.

📈 CHART OF THE DAY

Existing tenants accounted for over one-third of owner-user office purchases in Q3, up from less than 10% in late 2025, as falling prices and loan distress fueled discounted deals.

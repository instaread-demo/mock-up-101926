How Toulon Golf’s new putters are taking the alignment system to a whole new level

By James Hogg, Senior Equipment Writer
Follow Today's Golfer on Google

Published: 15 October 2026

Most putters alignment markings are a design feature but Toulon are changing that with their Concept 59 Daytona Beach collection. Here’s how…

We’re no strangers to Toulon Golf at Today’s Golfer – not because we share initials, but as huge fans of their collections since regaining independence in 2023. That being said, the latest collection of putters features something even more stand-out and different compared to what we’ve seen from them in recent years.

The Concept 59 Daytona Beach follows a similar path as the Formula Series that launched in summer 2025 in that there is one head, but for this release it’s all about the alignment and hosel configuration over a changing rear shape.

Most of the best putters feature a useful alignment system, but Toulon have gone one step farther by using an alignment marking that is designed to complement the movement attributed with the hosel.

But how did Toulon get here?

Concept 59 isn’t just a name, it’s Toulon Golf’s platform for exploration: a place to test theories, materials, constructions, and fitting concepts without being constrained by convention.

Honoring the founder, Sean Toulon, who was born in 1959, the Concept series represents a lifetime spent challenging accepted ideas in golf club design and searching for better ways to build equipment.

The idea is simple: question everything, test everything, and follow where the results lead.

What does that mean for Daytona Beach?

Those familiar with Toulon Golf will quickly recognize the body of the Daytona Beach, considering it’s been a part of the family line-up since 2022.

Its shape carries subtle echoes of one of Toulon’s most recognizable designs from earlier in his career but was reshaped by his son, Joe, after working extensively with tour players over the last half-decade.

To concentrate more mass forward and toward the heel and toe, a precisely milled 304 stainless steel front section has been combined with a lightweight 6061 aluminum rear section to create a muti-material construction that is stable while keeping the center of gravity closer to the face. Toulon have opted for the same Deep Diamond Mill face pattern as that of the 2026 Collection.

The main focus of the Concept 59 Daytona Beach is the alignment system, however.

Whether you’re using a blade, a mallet, or low-torque putter, different alignment options appear across different models without a clear explanation for why.

Dot, short or long line, T-square, and two-ball – we know how important these markings can be for golfers. But why?

Daytona Beach is about answering that.

The company, therefore, embarked on an exploration of the relationship between a golfer’s stroke, the neck of the putter, how much the face is designed to rotate, and what the golfer sees at address.

Model specifications

The theory they came up with is that putters designed for more face rotation should feature less invasive alignment, and it led them to create the Concept 59 Daytona Beach range with a deliberate progression. More rotation meant less visual influence. Less rotation meant more visual influence.

As the intended rotation decreases, the alignment system becomes progressively more prominent, providing the golfer with more visual structure and directional cues. That theory has been put into practice with five distinct configurations:

H4.5: A short slant neck designed for the golfer who naturally creates more face rotation throughout the stroke and prefers to feel the putter release.

To complement that movement, the alignment system is intentionally minimal. A single sight dot provides a subtle reference point without introducing a strong linear cue that could visually interfere with the natural rotation of the putter.

It is Daytona Beach in its purest form: more rotation, less visual influence. For the golfer who wants freedom in the stroke and a clean, unobstructed look at address, H4.5 provides exactly that.

H1: Taking a step towards the middle of the Daytona Beach rotation spectrum, the H1 is traditional plumber’s neck is designed for a golfer who still creates meaningful face rotation, but less than the H4.5 offers.

The alignment system also evolves accordingly. A sight dot is paired with a short line, introducing additional directional information while remaining relatively quiet behind the ball.

That balance is what defines the H1. There is enough visual structure to help establish the face and intended starting line, without allowing alignment to dominate what the golfer sees. It is a familiar neck configuration approached through an entirely new alignment philosophy.

Double Bend: Continuing the progression toward less face rotation and greater visual influence, the Double Bend configuration is designed for a more face-balanced delivery, allowing the putter to remain more stable and rotate less throughout the stroke.

As that rotation decreases, Toulon introduces a stronger alignment cue: a long sightline, extending through the rear section of the putter, giving the golfer a more pronounced reference for establishing direction and setting the putter to the intended starting line.

Formula 45: Moving further toward the low-rotation end of the Daytona Beach spectrum, this center-mounted shaft introduces a different relationship between it, the golfer, and the head’s CG.

Designed for reduced face rotation, Formula 45 is paired with a more substantial alignment system to complement that movement, featuring a strong T-shape that provides both a target-oriented line for additional visual structure, as well as a strong perpendicular reference to the face.

Formula 45 represents the point where alignment becomes a dominant part of the visual presentation, intentionally matched to a putter designed to rotate significantly less.

Formula 90: Representing the opposite end of the Daytona Beach spectrum from H4.5, this setup is designed for the least amount of face rotation in the family. Another center-shaft, like the 45, is paired with the most visually influential alignment system of the five models.

A long T-shaped alignment feature has been introduced to create the strongest relationship between the target line, putter face and center of the golf ball.

Everything about Formula 90 is designed to provide structure. Where H4.5 minimizes visual information to complement greater rotation, Formula 90 embraces it.

Availability

Between all five options available, the Concept 59 Daytona Beach theory is illustrated: as rotation decreases, visual guidance increases, helping golfers find a combination of movement and alignment that better matches the way they putt.

Rather than treating the neck and alignment system as two independent choices, Daytona Beach connects them.

The neck helps define how the putter is intended to move, while the alignment markings are designed to complement that movement.

It’s the simple idea that lies at the heart of Concept 59: take something that has traditionally been arbitrary, question and why, develop the understanding, and build a putter specifically to test that theory.

Ultimately, Daytona Beach is about giving golfers a putter that works more naturally with the way they see the line and move the putter.
